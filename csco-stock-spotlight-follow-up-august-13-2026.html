CSCO Follow-Up: 50-Day SMA Fails — 100-Day SMA Comes Into Focus

Cisco delivered strong results and upbeat guidance, but the market reaction was sharply negative. With the daily 50-SMA now lost, the technical roadmap has changed.

Strong numbers, weak reaction

Cisco's fiscal fourth-quarter revenue rose 17.6% year over year to about $17.25 billion. The company guided fiscal 2027 revenue to $72.2 billion–$73.4 billion, above Wall Street expectations reported by Reuters, while projecting $7.5 billion in fiscal 2027 AI infrastructure revenue from hyperscalers.

Yet the stock sold off hard. One reason investors may have focused on is profitability: Cisco's first-quarter adjusted gross-margin outlook of 65%–66% came in slightly below consensus, with a more hardware-heavy product mix and higher component costs pressuring margins. Cisco had also entered earnings after a powerful 2026 run, leaving expectations elevated.

Analysts remained constructive

The selloff came even as several analysts stayed bullish. Rosenblatt reiterated Buy and raised its target from $150 to $165. KeyBanc maintained Overweight and lifted its target from $130 to $135, while BofA kept a Buy rating and $150 target.

The analyst commentary highlighted broad revenue strength, 28% year-over-year Networking growth, strong hyperscaler demand, and guidance that some analysts viewed as conservative. That contrast is worth watching: positive fundamental commentary does not guarantee positive price action.

Dagger Takeaway: Good news does not automatically mean a stock goes higher. The market's reaction matters. When price rejects strong headlines, let the chart confirm what buyers are actually willing to defend.

The 50-day SMA failed to hold

In the previous CSCO Spotlight, the daily 50-SMA was the key support test. That test failed. CSCO closed Thursday at $113.47, leaving the stock below the moving average after the post-earnings selloff.

That does not automatically mean the stock has to continue lower. A bounce could bring the daily 50-SMA back into play — this time as a potential resistance test. A strong reclaim and hold would improve the short-term technical picture. A rejection from underneath would suggest the former support level is beginning to act as resistance.

The 100-day SMA comes into focus

If selling pressure continues, attention shifts toward the daily 100-SMA around $106.37. The level is not a prediction or a guaranteed bounce point. It is simply the next major technical area where buyers may have an opportunity to show themselves.

If CSCO reaches that area and buyers defend it, the stock could begin stabilizing. If the 100-SMA also fails to hold, the technical picture would weaken further.

What I'm watching next

The near-term setup is now fairly straightforward: watch for a bounce into the broken 50-SMA and see whether it is reclaimed or rejected. If CSCO cannot regain that area and selling resumes, the 100-SMA near $106.37 becomes increasingly important.

The underlying business results remain strong, but the chart has weakened in the short term. There is no need to assume either the selloff is finished or that further downside is guaranteed.

Wait for confirmation, not assumption.
