CEG Surges on Google Nuclear Deal: The Levels That Matter Next

Published October 6, 2026

New Spotlight • October 6, 2026

A landmark Google deal puts CEG and the AI power story back in focus

30-SECOND SUMMARY

What matters most

  • Constellation Energy surged 12.25% Tuesday to close at $300.40 after announcing a major long-term nuclear-power agreement with Google.
  • The agreement includes a 20-year PPA supporting 890 MW of new nuclear capacity from upgrades at 11 reactors, backed by more than $4.3 billion of planned Constellation investment.
  • Google and Constellation also signed a separate 15-year, 2,700 MW energy-supply agreement in PJM, bringing the broader commercial arrangement to roughly 3.6 GW.
  • CEG reached $309.80 intraday before cooling. Near term, $295.46 is the first support I’m watching, while a recovery back toward $309.80 would put the session high back in focus.
  • The larger chart is centered on the $298.94–$298.95 structural pivot. Below it, stronger confluence (multiple technical levels clustering around the same price) appears around $286.59–$287.02, with larger structural support near $271.16–$272.26.

What happened?

CEG jumped 12.25% Tuesday and closed at $300.40 after Constellation and Google unveiled a sweeping long-term agreement aimed at adding reliable power to the PJM grid as electricity demand from data centers and other large users continues to grow. The stock traded as high as $309.80 before giving back part of the move late in the session.

The reaction was bigger than CEG alone. Other power and nuclear-related names rallied as investors treated the agreement as another sign that Big Tech is willing to commit long-term capital to secure electricity for the AI buildout. For CEG, the move also came after a difficult year: the stock entered Tuesday down roughly 24% for 2026.

Latest News

Google backs a major nuclear expansion: The 20-year agreement will support upgrades at 11 Constellation-owned nuclear units in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey. Those upgrades are expected to add 890 MW of new capacity, roughly comparable to the output of a large reactor. Constellation plans to invest more than $4.3 billion, with the first uprate expected in 2028.

The relationship extends beyond the 890 MW expansion: Google and Constellation also entered a separate 15-year agreement covering another 2,700 MW in PJM. The companies established a framework to explore additional generation, storage and demand-response opportunities, while Constellation expanded its Google Cloud and Gemini Enterprise technology relationship.

Amazon adds to the Big Tech pattern: On September 30, Constellation announced a separate 20-year agreement with Amazon covering 690 MW at Maryland's Calvert Cliffs plant, including about 190 MW of new nuclear output. That agreement is expected to support more than $3 billion of infrastructure investment. Constellation also has long-term nuclear relationships with Microsoft and Meta.

Wall Street Reaction

Wall Street remains generally constructive, but expectations are not uniform. Bernstein reiterated an Outperform rating Tuesday with a $296 price target. That target sits near where CEG traded after the initial surge, so the positive rating does not imply large additional upside from current levels by itself.

BMO Capital maintained Outperform on October 2 while lowering its target to $350 from $379. FactSet data cited with that update showed an average Buy rating and a mean analyst target of $345.63. Analyst targets are opinions, not guarantees, and the wide range reinforces why price confirmation still matters.

Key Risks to Watch

The first risk is the size of Tuesday's move. A 12% one-day surge can attract follow-through, but it can also invite profit-taking and sharp reversals, especially if buyers cannot hold the levels reclaimed during the breakout.

The fundamental story also unfolds over years, not days. The first Google-backed nuclear uprate is expected in 2028, with additional work extending into the early 2030s. Large infrastructure projects carry execution, regulatory and cost risks. The commercial pricing and eventual earnings contribution of the Google agreements were not fully disclosed, so the headline size of the deal should not be treated as an immediate earnings forecast.

The Roadmap

Upside: If CEG can stabilize above the $298.94–$298.95 structural pivot, Tuesday's $309.80 intraday high is the first major hurdle. A clean move through that level brings $313.58–$316.68 into focus as the next major upside area. Above that, $320.66–$322.10 is the next major higher resistance area, followed by $325.26–$328.69 before $342.39–$342.85, one of the most important higher levels on the larger chart.

Downside: $295.46 is the first support below the structural pivot, followed by $292.96 as the next near-term support. If $292.96 fails, the roadmap shifts toward the stronger confluence areas at $286.59–$287.02 and $279.17–$279.43. The major $271.16–$272.26 structural support area is especially important on the larger chart. If that structure fails, $256.67–$257.37 is the next deeper major support area.

What matters next?

The first question is whether Tuesday's move can turn into a durable breakout rather than a one-day reaction to a major headline. CEG does not need to immediately make another high. Holding the $295–$299 region and rebuilding above the structural pivot would be constructive. Repeated rejection below that area would shift attention toward the lower support roadmap.

Beyond price, investors will be watching how quickly Constellation converts its expanding list of Big Tech agreements into additional capacity, contracted revenue and earnings growth. The Google deal strengthens the long-term AI-power narrative, but execution will matter.

Dagger Trading Take

The catalyst is real. Now the chart has to prove the breakout can hold.

Google's agreement adds another major technology customer to a Constellation story that already includes Amazon, Microsoft and Meta. That helps explain why investors reacted so strongly Tuesday.

But a strong story does not eliminate technical risk after a 12% move. The $298.94–$298.95 structural pivot is the area I care about most around $300, with $295.46 the first support underneath. If buyers can defend this region, the higher roadmap stays relevant. If the breakout fades, the lower support levels become much more important.

Momentum is key. Stay sharp.
