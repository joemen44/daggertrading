NKE: Nike Breaks Down After Earnings. Where Does Support Come From Now?

Published October 1, 2026

New Spotlight • October 1, 2026

Nike's quarter was mixed. The outlook was the bigger problem.

30-SECOND SUMMARY

What matters most

  • Nike reported fiscal Q1 revenue of $11.21 billion, down 4% year over year, with diluted EPS of $0.48.
  • The bigger issue was the outlook: Nike expects fiscal 2027 revenue to decline by a high-single-digit percentage and adjusted EPS of $1.15–$1.35.
  • Greater China revenue fell 22% reported / 26% currency-neutral, while Converse revenue fell 28%.
  • Management is repositioning Nike Sportswear, Jordan Brand and Greater China while launching its Pace restructuring program, targeting about $2.5 billion in cumulative savings through fiscal 2031.
  • After closing at $35.15, NKE fell to roughly $32 after hours. The first job is stabilization, not guessing a bottom.

What happened?

Nike's fiscal first quarter was not a clean disaster. Gross margin improved, costs came down and earnings per share held up better than the revenue trend might suggest. But investors were handed a much weaker picture of what comes next.

Management now expects revenue to decline by a high-single-digit percentage for fiscal 2027. That is a much tougher backdrop for a stock that was already in a long-term downtrend before earnings. NKE closed the regular session at $35.15 and then broke sharply lower after hours toward the $32 area.

Latest News / Earnings

Revenue remains under pressure: Nike reported $11.21 billion in quarterly revenue, down 4% year over year. Nike Brand revenue was $10.95 billion, also down 4%, while Converse fell 28% to $263 million.

China remains a major problem area: Greater China revenue fell 22% on a reported basis and 26% on a currency-neutral basis. Reuters reported that this marked a ninth consecutive quarter of declining China sales. On the earnings call, management said the cleanup will take multiple seasons, while Nike is also tightening digital distribution and marketplace discipline in the region.

The turnaround is becoming a restructuring story: Nike introduced Pace, an operating-model transformation that includes supply-chain modernization, a realignment from four geographic regions to three and further organizational streamlining. The company expects roughly $2.5 billion in cumulative savings through fiscal 2031, alongside about $1 billion in pretax charges over that period. Additional job reductions are expected as the program develops.

Direct-to-consumer weakness is another pressure point: Nike Direct revenue fell 8% reported, including a 13% decline in Nike Brand Digital and a 5% decline in Nike-owned stores.

There are still signs of progress: North America grew 2%, gross margin expanded 60 basis points to 42.8%, inventories fell 3%, and management said its performance business continues to show momentum across sports including running, football, training and basketball. The recovery is not failing everywhere at once; it is uneven.

Why the outlook mattered more than the headline quarter

Nike expects fiscal 2027 revenue to decline in the high-single digits and adjusted diluted EPS of $1.15–$1.35. The earnings call also made clear that some of the pressure from restructuring troubled parts of the business could continue through the rest of fiscal 2027 and bleed into fiscal 2028.

That changes the question for the stock. This is no longer just about whether one quarter beat or missed estimates. Traders are trying to judge how long the turnaround takes, how much revenue Nike is willing to sacrifice while repairing distribution and product positioning, and when those actions begin producing durable growth again.

What management says is broken

Three areas repeatedly came up: Nike Sportswear, Jordan Brand and Greater China. Sportswear represents a large part of the business and management acknowledged weakness in older lifestyle franchises. Nike is deliberately reducing some high-volume products, including Dunks, while trying to create more newness and energy.

Jordan is also being managed more carefully after management said the market had been oversupplied with some retro products. The company plans to reduce the volume and frequency of selected launches in an effort to restore scarcity. In China, Nike is cutting some digital distribution, cleaning up inventory and repositioning the marketplace rather than chasing near-term volume.

A symbol of how far sentiment has shifted

Reuters also noted that Nike was recently removed from the S&P 100 after an 18-year run. That index change did not cause the earnings selloff, but it is another reminder of how dramatically the market's view of the company has changed during the long decline.

Key Risks to Watch

The most obvious risk is that the turnaround takes longer than investors expect while revenue continues to shrink. Greater China remains weak, lifestyle demand is uneven, competition is intense and Nike is deliberately removing some volume as it tries to improve the health of its franchises.

The other side matters too. Expectations are already depressed and short interest has risen substantially. A genuinely better product cycle, improving China trends or stronger-than-expected execution could produce sharp upside reactions. That is why the roadmap below is built for both directions.

Updated Key Levels I'm Watching

NKE is moving through price territory it has not had to navigate in years. These levels were built by comparing multiple long-term, intermediate and short-term structures, then looking for areas where those structures and current pivots cluster. The goal is not to call the bottom. It is to identify where price may react and what NKE would need to reclaim before the technical picture begins to improve.

Immediate Decision Area$32.00–$32.42NKE finished after hours near this area. Holding and building above it would be the first sign of stabilization; losing it keeps the lower roadmap in play.

The Roadmap

If buyers stabilize the stock: Start with $32.00–$32.42. Above that, $32.59–$32.75 is the first step, followed by the $33.15–$33.65 reclaim area. A stronger recovery would then have to deal with $33.90–$34.20 and the $35.04–$35.08 area that broke during the earnings reaction. Above that, $38.50 becomes the first major macro recovery test.

If sellers stay in control: A decisive loss of the $32 area shifts attention toward $30.29–$30.76. Below that, the roadmap becomes much wider: $27.61, then $24.39, with $20.61 reserved as a deep macro reference rather than a prediction.

Where does support come from when a stock is this broken?

When a stock falls into territory it has not traded in for years, recent horizontal support becomes harder to find. That is where traders can compare older price structure, larger swing relationships, shorter-term extensions and current pivots to identify areas where multiple references point to roughly the same place.

That still does not make those areas guaranteed floors. A stock can be extremely oversold and remain in a downtrend. A bounce is not the same thing as a repaired trend, and a famous brand is not automatically a reason to buy a falling stock.

What matters next?

For the next session, the first question is simple: can NKE actually stabilize around $32 after the earnings shock? If it can, the reclaim roadmap gives buyers a series of tests before the chart begins to look meaningfully healthier. If it cannot, the lower roadmap becomes more important.

Beyond price, watch management's execution in Greater China, Sportswear and Jordan, whether North American and performance-product momentum continues, and whether future guidance shows the expected revenue pressure beginning to ease.

Dagger Trading Take

Down a lot does not mean done going down.

Nike is a globally recognized company, but the chart and the business are both going through a major reset. The quarter contained some encouraging pieces, yet management's outlook says the turnaround will take time and may require more near-term pain.

That makes this a good example of why traders should separate price being low from price proving it can stabilize. Let the nearest level do its job, watch the reaction and expand the roadmap only when price earns it.

Momentum is key. Stay sharp.
