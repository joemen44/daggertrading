Weekly Market Wrap: September 28-October 2, 2026

Jobs • Treasury Yields • MU • INTC • NKE • Trading Psychology

The Week in One Thought

The market survived another surge in yields... then weak jobs data changed the conversation.

Stocks spent much of the week fighting rising Treasury yields, elevated oil prices and renewed inflation concerns. By Friday, however, a much weaker-than-expected jobs report gave traders a different reason to buy: expectations for another Federal Reserve rate hike dropped sharply.

The result was a mixed week underneath the surface. The Nasdaq managed to finish higher, while the S&P 500 and Dow ended lower.

The Market: Friday Changed the Tone

Friday delivered the strongest session of the week. The S&P 500 gained 0.73%, the Nasdaq climbed 1.19% and the Dow rose 0.49% after September's jobs report came in well below expectations.

+0.45%Nasdaq for the week

That split tells the story pretty well. Technology remained resilient, but the broader market continued to wrestle with rates, oil and uncertainty about the economy.

The Big Story

Treasury yields kept testing the market... until jobs data gave stocks some relief.

The 10-year Treasury yield briefly climbed above 5.34% Thursday, reaching its highest level since 2002. That's difficult for stocks to ignore. Higher yields increase borrowing costs and give investors a more attractive alternative to equities, particularly when stock valuations are already elevated.

Yet stocks didn't completely break down. AI enthusiasm remained a source of strength, and when Friday's jobs report reduced expectations for another near-term Fed hike, buyers returned quickly.

Jobs Report: Only 29,000 Added

September nonfarm payrolls increased by just 29,000, far below expectations. Previous months were also revised lower.

The immediate market reaction wasn't simply “weak jobs are good.” Traders focused on what the report could mean for interest rates. Expectations for another Fed hike at the October meeting fell sharply, helping rate-sensitive stocks and technology rebound.

That's the balancing act heading into October: a cooling labor market may reduce pressure on the Fed, but too much weakness would create a different problem.

Dagger Trading Spotlights This Week

MU, INTC and NKE gave traders three very different setups this week: huge earnings with a complicated reaction, a momentum run into major resistance, and a post-earnings breakdown searching for support.

MU: Huge Numbers, Complicated Reaction

Micron reported record fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $54.23 billion, while non-GAAP earnings reached $33.42 per share. AI-driven memory demand remains at the heart of the story.

But MU also gave traders another reminder that strong earnings don't guarantee a simple stock reaction. The initial response was volatile, making the chart and the reaction around important levels just as important as the headline numbers.

INTC: A Big Rally Finally Hit Resistance

Intel entered the week with powerful momentum, but the rally ran directly into a major technical test. INTC reached $130.24, just 16 cents below the $130.40 major resistance area mapped on the chart, before reversing sharply.

That rejection forced the roadmap to change. Instead of chasing the previous move, the focus shifted toward whether INTC could stabilize, reclaim its first recovery areas and eventually make another attempt at the highs. It's a good example of why resistance matters most when price actually reaches it.

NKE: Earnings Put the Turnaround Back Under the Microscope

Nike became the newest Dagger Trading Spotlight after earnings. Fiscal first-quarter revenue fell 4% to $11.2 billion, and the company said it expects fiscal 2027 revenue to decline by a high-single-digit percentage as weakness in Greater China and other parts of the business continues to weigh on the turnaround.

The stock sold off again Friday, leaving traders with a much different question than the one surrounding MU or INTC: where does support finally begin to matter? The new NKE Spotlight maps the first stabilization area near $32, followed by the reclaim zones that would need to be recovered before the technical picture begins to improve.

What the Week Taught Us

This week gave traders three very different examples. Micron delivered enormous numbers, but the stock reaction wasn't straightforward. Intel carried tremendous momentum into resistance... and resistance mattered. Nike delivered bad news and broke lower, shifting the focus from momentum to stabilization.

Meanwhile, the broader market spent the week reacting to oil, Treasury yields, economic data and changing expectations for the Fed. That's why the headline is only the beginning.

Watch the levels. Watch the reaction. Momentum is key.

Trading Psychology

Control the decision, not the next candle.

A strong earnings report can fall. A weak economic report can send stocks higher. A stock with enormous momentum can reverse when it reaches resistance.

You don't control any of that. What you can control is whether you chase, whether you respect your risk and whether you wait for the market to confirm what you think should happen.

Going Into Next Week

The jobs report helped. The yield problem hasn't disappeared.

Friday reduced expectations for another immediate Fed hike, but long-term Treasury yields remain historically high and the market is entering the fourth quarter with several competing forces. AI spending continues to support parts of technology. At the same time, investors are watching economic growth, inflation, oil prices and a bond market that has repeatedly challenged equity valuations.

The question next week isn't simply whether stocks can rally. It's whether Friday's relief can turn into sustained momentum.
