MU: Where Does Micron Find Support After the Sharp Pullback?

Originally Published August 19, 2026 • Updated August 19, 2026

Market Update August 19, 2026 • Early Afternoon

Price Action + News

MU Bounces From Support, but Resistance Remains

MU fell into the $916–$920 area overnight, where buyers stepped in and pushed the stock as high as roughly $968.65. The move carried MU above its daily 50-day moving average near $962, but it did not hold.

Sellers stepped back in around the market open, and MU moved lower again. By early Wednesday afternoon, the stock was trading back around the low-$930s.

Wednesday's News

Cathie Wood said ARK is avoiding memory stocks because unusually high memory prices could eventually encourage chipmakers to reduce their reliance on high-bandwidth memory.

At the same time, Micron's current business remains strong. TrendForce data cited by Barron's showed Micron's NAND market share increased from 13.9% to 15.1% in the second quarter, while NAND revenue increased 99.2% from the previous quarter.

What I'm Watching Now

$932.64 remains the first important level. If MU can move back above it and hold with improving momentum, the short-term picture would improve. Above that, the daily 50-day moving average remains an important hurdle.

If weakness continues, I'm still watching the $921–$917 support area, followed by $908–$910 below it.

Dagger Trading Take: Buyers have shown interest near support, but MU still hasn't proven that the pullback is over. For now, I'm watching the levels and waiting for confirmation.

What happened?

Micron closed Tuesday at $941.10, down 7%, ending a five-day winning streak that had pushed the stock above $1,000 on Monday.

The selloff wasn't just about MU. Chip and AI-related stocks were weak across the market. Rising Treasury yields added pressure, while investors also took profits after a strong run in many technology names.

Why MU is still interesting

Short-term momentum has weakened, but the bigger memory and AI story hasn't disappeared. Demand for memory products remains strong, which creates an interesting setup: strong longer-term demand, but a stock that is currently pulling back.

There is also a new concern around the memory sector. Cathie Wood said ARK is avoiding memory stocks, arguing that unusually high memory prices and shortages could eventually push chipmakers toward designs that rely less on high-bandwidth memory.

There is another side to that argument. Memory supply is expected to remain tight, AI-related demand remains strong, and Micron has signed long-term customer agreements representing roughly $100 billion in cumulative revenue through 2030.

For MU, it adds another question for investors: how long can today's strong memory-demand cycle last?

Instead of trying to guess where MU will bottom, I'm watching how it reacts around a few important price areas.

Three areas I'm watching

First Key Level $932.64 This level was important during Tuesday's trading and continued getting reactions into extended hours. If MU gets back above it and holds, that would be a positive first step.
Key Support $921–$917 If MU stays below $932.64, this is the next area where I'll be watching to see if buyers step in.
Deeper Support $908–$910 If selling continues, this is another important area that could become a bigger test for buyers.

What would improve the chart?

The first thing I'd like to see is MU move back above $932.64 and hold that area as support. That wouldn't automatically mean the pullback is over, but it would be an encouraging sign that buyers are starting to regain control.

What would concern me?

If MU keeps getting rejected below $932.64, I'd continue watching the lower support areas. A break below $921–$917 would put more attention on $908–$910.

Dagger Trading Take

Watch the reaction

MU has gone from strong momentum to a sharp pullback very quickly. I'm not trying to predict the exact bottom.

$932.64 is the first level I'm watching. Below that, $921–$917 and then $908–$910 are the areas that stand out.

The important part isn't simply reaching a level. It's how the stock reacts when it gets there.

Wait for confirmation, not assumption.
