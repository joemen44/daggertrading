Weekly Market Wrap: September 14-18, 2026

Fed • Rates • Oil • DELL • ORCL • SPCX • AVGO

The Week in One Thought

The Fed finally moved, but AI and semiconductors kept fighting back.

It was a volatile week for stocks as the Federal Reserve raised interest rates, Treasury yields pushed above 5% and oil remained above $100 a barrel. Even with those macro pressures, strength in semiconductors helped the Nasdaq finish the week higher while the broader market struggled.

The Market: Rates, Oil and the Fed Drove the Week

Friday ended quietly compared with the rest of the week. The S&P 500 gained 0.17%, the Nasdaq rose 0.40% and the Dow slipped 0.18%. For the full week, the S&P 500 finished with only a small loss, the Nasdaq ended higher, and the Dow posted its largest weekly percentage decline since March.

The Big Story

The Fed raised rates for the first time in more than three years.

The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate by 25 basis points Wednesday as inflation remained a concern. Markets now have to weigh the possibility of additional tightening against a still-resilient economy and continued investment in AI infrastructure.

Oil and Yields Kept the Pressure On

Crude oil remained above $100 a barrel late in the week, keeping inflation concerns alive. At the same time, benchmark Treasury yields moved above 5%. That combination created a difficult backdrop for equities and helped keep traders sensitive to every move in rates and energy prices.

Semiconductors Helped Tech Fight Back

Despite the macro pressure, semiconductor strength helped the Nasdaq outperform into Friday. That split was one of the clearest themes of the week: the broader market remained cautious, while selected AI and semiconductor names continued to attract buyers.

What the Week Taught Us

This week was another reminder that the market does not have to move as one. Rates, oil and inflation created pressure across the broader market, but individual AI and semiconductor names still produced strong moves. That is why the reaction at key levels matters just as much as the headline.

Watch the levels. Watch the reaction. Momentum is key.

Going Into Next Week

The Fed decision is behind us. The rate path is not.

With the September decision complete, attention now shifts to what comes next. Treasury yields, oil prices and comments from Fed officials can remain important market drivers, while traders continue watching whether strength in semiconductors can broaden out to the rest of the market.
