Trading Psychology

Revenge Trading: How to Stop Chasing Your Losses

Simple, practical trading education focused on process, risk and better decision-making.

Revenge trading is the attempt to quickly recover money after a loss. The trader stops treating the next setup as an independent decision and starts treating it as a way to get back to even.

It can happen after one large loss or after several small losses. The amount is not what defines revenge trading. The important change is the motivation behind the next trade.

What revenge trading can look like

  • Increasing position size immediately after losing.
  • Entering without waiting for confirmation.
  • Taking a setup you normally would reject.
  • Re-entering the same stock repeatedly because you want to be right.
  • Ignoring a stop because taking another loss feels unacceptable.
  • Thinking more about today's P&L than the actual chart.

Why losses create urgency

A realized loss feels final. Taking another trade can make the loss feel temporary because there is now a chance to recover it. That is where the danger begins: the new trade may be chosen for emotional relief rather than because the market is offering a high-quality opportunity.

The market has no relationship with your previous trade. A stock does not become a better setup because you are down money.

Create a break between loss and action

One of the simplest defenses is a predefined reset. After a loss that affects your emotions, step away from the order button long enough to evaluate the next setup independently. The reset does not need to be dramatic. The purpose is to interrupt the automatic urge to respond.

When you return, ask: Would I take this exact trade if I were flat on the day? If the answer is no, the trade may be about recovery rather than opportunity.

Reduce risk when your judgment changes

If you notice frustration, anger or urgency, reducing size—or deciding not to trade—can protect you while your decision-making is compromised. Position size should never increase simply because you want to recover faster.

Important: A stop is not a challenge from the market. It is the point where your original trade idea no longer meets the plan you defined.

Accept that recovery does not have a deadline

A trader can make a reasonable decision and still lose. Trying to erase that loss immediately often creates the conditions for a larger one. Recovery can happen over many future trades. There is no requirement that it happen today.

This shifts the goal from “get my money back” to “make the next good decision.” That is a much more controllable objective.

🗡️ Dagger Takeaway: The next trade does not owe you the previous loss. Reset the decision. Define the setup, confirmation, invalidation and risk as if the earlier trade never happened.
