Trading Psychology

Revenge Trading: Stop Chasing Your Losses

The next trade owes you nothing.

Revenge trading begins when the next trade stops being an independent decision and becomes a mission to recover the previous loss.
The next trade owes you nothing.

1. How the Pattern Develops

1. Planned Trade
🎯

You take a valid setup.

2. Loss
📉

The trade fails and money is gone.

3. Anger
😠

The loss feels personal.

4. Get It Back
💢

Recovery becomes the new goal.

5. Lower Standards
⚠️

You force another entry.

6. Spiral
🌀

More risk creates more emotion and more mistakes.

Small decisions become habits. Protect the process early.

2. Process vs. Emotion

Disciplined ProcessEmotional Process
Next trade✓ Independent decision✕ Recovery attempt
Position size✓ Based on risk plan✕ Increased to make it back
Confirmation✓ Still required✕ Ignored because of urgency
Stop✓ Respected✕ Moved or removed
Mindset✓ Can accept another loss✕ Needs the next trade to win
Time horizon✓ Recovery has no deadline✕ Must get even today

3. Quick Reset

  • Pause: Is this trade actually in the plan?
  • Risk: Is size defined before entry?
  • Evidence: What confirms the setup?
  • Invalidation: Where am I wrong?
  • Choice: Can I comfortably skip this trade?
If you cannot answer those questions clearly, waiting is a valid decision.

4. Outcome ≠ Decision Quality

Reset + No Trade = Good Decision
📉 → 🛑 → ✅

Stopping after a loss can be active risk management, not weakness.

Chase + Win = Bad Reinforcement
😠 → 📈 → ❌

Getting the money back can reward the exact behavior that eventually causes a larger loss.

Grade the decision first. Grade the outcome second.

5. Warning Signs

  • Increasing size immediately after a loss
  • Re-entering the same stock repeatedly
  • Entering before confirmation
  • Thinking about getting back to green
  • Moving or ignoring a stop
  • Feeling anger or urgency at the order button
When emotion changes the plan, reduce risk and reset.
🗡️

6. Key Takeaway

Your edge needs rules around it. The goal is not perfect execution—it is noticing when you drift and returning to the process.

“The next trade owes you nothing.”
7. Go Deeper (optional reading)

The deeper lesson

A realized loss can create urgency because another trade makes recovery feel possible. But the market has no relationship with your previous result. A useful reset question is: “Would I take this exact trade if I were flat on the day?” If the answer is no, the trade may be about emotional recovery rather than opportunity.

A simple practice

Before the next order, write down the setup, confirmation, invalidation and risk. After the trade, review whether you followed them. That keeps the review focused on behavior you can actually control.
