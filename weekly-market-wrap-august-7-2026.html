Weekly Market Wrap: August 3-7, 2026

Jobs, Fed Expectations, Earnings & SPCX

The Week in One Thought

The reaction mattered more than the headline.

A surprisingly weak jobs report might normally raise concerns about the economy. Instead, stocks rallied as traders focused on what weaker hiring could mean for interest rates. SPCX offered another example: a huge number of previously restricted shares became eligible for sale, but buyers stepped in rather than allowing the feared selloff to take control.

Stocks Finished a Strong Week

Wall Street ended the week on a strong note. The S&P 500 gained about 3.6%, the Nasdaq rose roughly 5.2%, and the Dow added about 3.0%. The S&P 500 also finished Friday at a record closing high.

The bigger story was why stocks kept climbing. Investors were balancing signs of a slowing economy against the possibility that slower growth could reduce pressure on the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates.

The Jobs Report Changed the Conversation

Friday's July employment report was the week's biggest scheduled economic event. The U.S. economy unexpectedly lost 23,000 jobs in July, while economists had expected job growth. Earlier months were also revised lower.

That was concerning for the economy, but the stock market focused on another possibility: weaker hiring could give the Federal Reserve more reason to hold off on raising interest rates. Expectations for a September rate increase fell after the report, and stocks rallied Friday.

Dagger Take

Watch the reaction, not just the headline.

A weak economic report doesn't automatically mean stocks must fall. A strong report doesn't automatically mean they must rise. What matters is how the new information changes expectations—and how buyers and sellers actually react.

Earnings Continued to Support the Market

Corporate earnings remained another important source of strength. By the end of the week, roughly 85% of S&P 500 companies that had reported results had beaten analysts' earnings expectations.

Technology and AI-related spending remained major themes, while strong company results helped support the broader market despite questions about inflation, interest rates and economic growth.

SPCX: The Lockup Everyone Was Watching

On August 6, roughly 911.5 million previously restricted SPCX shares became eligible to be sold following the company's first major lockup expiration.

Becoming eligible for sale does not mean all of those shares are immediately sold. Still, traders were understandably concerned about additional selling pressure.

Instead, buyers stepped in around the $109.20 area and SPCX finished the August 6 session green. The stock then continued higher, providing another example of why traders shouldn't assume they know how price will react to a known catalyst.

What the Week Taught Us

The common thread was simple: expectations and price reaction mattered as much as the news itself.

The jobs report was weak, yet stocks rallied as traders reconsidered interest rates. SPCX faced a potentially intimidating lockup expiration, yet buyers absorbed the initial pressure. Strong earnings also continued to support the market.

None of that means bad news is bullish or lockup expirations don't matter. It means the market often prices in expectations before an event happens. Once the news arrives, the actual reaction tells us what buyers and sellers are willing to do.

Looking Ahead

Prepare for the next move.

The market enters the new week near record highs, with Fed minutes, major retail earnings and several important technical levels ahead. SPCX also faces its next scheduled lockup expiration on August 20.
