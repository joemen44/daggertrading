Weekly Market Wrap: October 5–9, 2026

Jobs • Treasury Yields • MU • INTC • NKE • Trading Psychology

The Week in One Thought

New highs, a midweek shakeout, and a Friday recovery.

The major indexes ended higher, but the path was anything but smooth. Treasury yields, Fed policy expectations and questions around AI spending kept traders on their toes. Friday's rebound helped, but it did not erase the week's mixed signals.

The Market: Strength With Some Friction

The S&P 500 finished Friday at 7,811.54 and gained approximately 1.2% for the week. The Nasdaq Composite ended at 27,366.17, up approximately 0.6% for the week, while the Dow added approximately 0.9%. Small caps lagged, with the Russell 2000 down about 0.9% for the week.

+0.6%Nasdaq Composite for the week
−0.9%Russell 2000 for the week

Both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq made new highs earlier in the week before selling pressure returned. Friday's recovery was encouraging, but the weakness in smaller companies is a reminder that the rally has not been equally broad.

The Big Story

Fed minutes and higher yields kept pressure on the market.

Wednesday's release of the September Fed minutes reinforced the view that policymakers are still focused on inflation. The committee raised rates in September, and the minutes showed officials weighing the need for additional tightening. Treasury yields climbed during the week, creating another test for rate-sensitive stocks.

AI-related stocks also saw sharp swings as investors questioned whether spending and revenue expectations could keep pace with lofty valuations. Friday brought a rebound, but the market remains sensitive to both interest rates and AI headlines.

Economic Data: A Market Waiting for Inflation

Monday's ISM Services report showed continued expansion, while the week's Fed commentary kept rate expectations in focus. Friday's University of Michigan sentiment release underscored consumer unease. Next week's CPI and other inflation data could matter more than any single headline from this week.

Dagger Trading Spotlights This Week

Three Dagger Spotlights stood out this week: SPCX, CEG and ORCL. Each had a different catalyst, and each reminded traders why the reaction matters more than the headline alone.

SPCX: The Breakout and a Changing SpaceX Story

SPCX opened the week in focus after its recent strength and fresh technical developments. Later in the week, SpaceX's spectrum acquisition announcement added another reason for traders to watch the name. The bigger question now is whether buyers can defend the breakout or whether the stock needs to consolidate after its run.

CEG: A Google Nuclear Deal Puts Power Demand in Focus

Constellation Energy drew attention after its Google nuclear-power agreement, reinforcing the market's interest in electricity demand from AI and data centers. Strong headlines can bring momentum, but chasing a fast move without a plan can be costly. The Spotlight maps out the areas worth monitoring as price reacts.

Read the CEG Spotlight →

ORCL: AI Financing Questions Meet a Volatile Reaction

Oracle's AI ambitions remained a major talking point, but concerns about the financing and economics behind large infrastructure commitments added volatility. The stock saw pressure before recovering sharply Friday. The key is not to assume either side has won after one session; let price confirm the next move.

Read the ORCL Spotlight →

What the Week Taught Us

A strong theme is not the same as a clean trade. SPCX, CEG and ORCL all attracted attention, but the setup, entry and risk still matter. Meanwhile, the indexes showed that even a market making highs can deliver uncomfortable reversals.

Stay patient. Let the levels and the reaction do the talking. Momentum is key.

Going Into Next Week

CPI and earnings will test the rally.

September CPI is scheduled for Wednesday, with PPI and retail sales due Thursday. Major banks begin reporting, giving traders a first meaningful look at the next earnings season. Watch whether the major indexes can hold their recent gains and whether strength broadens beyond a narrow group of leaders.
